**Context:** Ongoing collaboration between Prof. Zhen Gao's group (SUSTech) and Prof. Mengyao Li's group (Tsinghua Shenzhen International Graduate School).

This project studies cavity-mediated topological transport in an **all-metal microwave metamaterial** rather than a conventional photonic crystal. Metallic resonant elements are placed inside a metallic cavity, where the surrounding electromagnetic environment modifies the effective interaction between dipolar modes. Depending on the strength of the cavity-mediated light–matter interaction, the system can exhibit **tunable band topology and cavity-controlled edge-state transport** — the topological phase is controlled not by physically changing the lattice after fabrication, but by the cavity environment itself (e.g., the cavity width or height).

## My role

My main contribution is on the experimental side: VNA-based transmission measurements, near-field scanning, field-amplitude/phase extraction, and **FFT-based reconstruction of measured dispersion spectra**, followed by comparison between measured spectra, simulated projected bands, and finite-structure field distributions to identify cavity-controlled edge-state transport.

**Significance.** This project extends my experience from photonic-crystal implementations to **cavity-mediated metamaterial and polaritonic platforms**, where the effective coupling is controlled by the electromagnetic environment rather than by fixed lattice geometry or material bias — a step toward reconfigurable topological wave systems.
